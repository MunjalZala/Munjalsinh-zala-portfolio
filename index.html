CanSat 2026 paraglider payload

As mechanical lead for Team Skybound, I designed a rocket-deployed payload that survives a 30G ejection, steers a paraglider to a GPS target and releases its cargo at 2 m. We placed 14th of 36 international teams, the only German university team in the field.

Mass
Cut from 1,400 g to 1,000 g through structural optimisation and material choice
Structure
PPA-CF load-bearing frame; TPU 95A nose cone and egg containment shell
Release
Nichrome burn-wire, 7 V transistor-switched, cutting a 0.85 mm nylon line in under 1 s; 1.5× safety factor
Steering
Two MG90S servos pulling the paraglider brake lines
Validation
ANSYS static, thermal and CFD at 30G; vacuum, thermal, drop, vibration and load tests
RoleMechanical Design Lead
EventCanSat, AIAA/AAS
LocationMonterey, Virginia
ToolsSolidWorks, ANSYS

Teach-by-demonstration robot arm

A hand-held leader arm with five potentiometers drives a six-joint follower over a direct radio link. You can record a motion and replay it, or steer the arm with hand gestures from a browser dashboard that shows a live 3D twin. Demonstrated live with every planned feature working.

Link
ESP-NOW between two ESP32s: commands at 50 Hz, status back at 20 Hz
Recording
Two 60 s slots at 25 poses per second, kept in flash through power-off
Motion
Trapezoidal velocity profile, 150 °/s and 625 °/s², driven through a PCA9685
Sensing
16-sample averaging, spike filter, EMA, dead zone and two-point calibration
Safety
Three e-stops, a 15° sync gate against start-up jumps, a 0.6 s link watchdog
Interface
Web Serial dashboard, three.js digital twin, offline MediaPipe gesture control
CourseEmbedded Systems Lab
Team2, with A. Khaladkar
DateNovember 2026
ToolsESP32, C++, three.js
StatusDemonstrated live
